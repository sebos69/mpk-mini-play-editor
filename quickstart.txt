User Guide
Introduction
The MPK mini Play mk3 Editor software gives you intuitive ways to edit the many functions that your
MPK mini Play mk3 can perform.

Support
For the latest information about this product (system requirements, compatibility information, etc.)
and product registration, visit akaipro.com.
For additional product support, visit akaipro.com/support.

Setup
To use the MPK mini Play mk3 Editor:
1.

Optional: Connect the unit to your computer using a standard USB cable. (If you are
connecting it to a USB hub, make sure it is a powered hub). You can still use the Editor and
create favorites without connecting an MPK mini Play mk3, but you will not be able to
send/load favorites to/from it.

2.

Open the Editor application on your computer. The window will show a visual representation of
all its editable controls. See Features > Graphical Interface for an overview of each section.

1.

Channel: These two fields determine the channels through which the pads, knobs, X-Y Controller, and
keyboard send MIDI data. Specific instructions are provided in the chapters for each control:
Operation > Editing a Pad’s MIDI Note, Editing a Knob, Editing the X-Y Controller (Joystick),
Editing the Keyboard.

2.

Sound: These two fields allow for selection of the internal sound that will be played by the keyboard
and the pads. See Operation > Editing the Sound to learn more.

3.

Keyboard Sound FX: This section allows you to control effects parameters for the internal sounds for
the keyboard. See Operation > Editing the Sound to learn more.

4.

Joystick: These settings control the parameters affected when moving the X-Y Controller along its X
axis (left/right) or Y axis (up/down). See Operation > Editing the X-Y Controller (Joystick) to learn
more.

5.

Arpeggiator: These settings control the timing, range, and general behavior of the arpeggiator. See
Operation > Editing the Arpeggiator to learn more.

6.

Favorites: These controls let you import a favorite from your computer or from your connected MPK
mini Play mk3. They also allow you to save the current settings as a favorite and export them to your
computer or to your connected device. See Operation > Managing Your Favorites to learn more.

7.

Pads: This section enables you to edit note assignments for the pads. 16 pads are pictured in the
Editor: 8 of them correspond to the pads on the MPK mini Play mk3 when Bank A is active, and the
other 8 correspond to the pads on the device when Bank B is active. This section also allows you to
toggle the Aftertouch mode for the pads. See Operation > Editing a Pad’s MIDI Note to learn more.

8.

Keyboard: This is a visual representation of the 25-key keyboard with fields that allow for editing of
the octave and transposition values. See Operation > Editing the Keyboard to learn more. To
change the key velocity sensitivity, see Operation > Editing the Keybed Curve.

9.

Knobs: This section enables you to edit the function of the knobs. 8 knobs are pictured in the Editor: 4
of them correspond to the knobs on the MPK mini Play mk3 when Bank A is active, and the other 4
correspond to the knobs on the device when Bank B is active. See Operation > Editing a Knob to
learn more.

Operation
Managing Your Favorites
With the Favorites feature, you can save all your current settings in the Editor to a single
.mpkminiplaymk3 file that can be loaded onto your MPK mini Play mk3. The device holds a
maximum of eight favorites, but you can store more favorites on your computer to load onto the unit
later with the Editor. This way, you can have countless different control configurations for different
situations. For instance, you may use different favorites with different software. Or you may use
some favorites for production and others for performance.

Saving Favorites
After saving a favorite on your computer, you can send it to your MPK or edit it again later. All
settings that you see in the Editor software will be saved in the favorite.
To edit the name of a favorite, click on the Preset field in the top right corner and type in the
desired name, which will be sent to the MPK mini Play mk3 along with the settings when clicking one
of the eight Send icons.
To save a favorite, go to File > Save Favorite. Alternatively, you can type Ctrl + S (Windows) or 
+ S (Mac). Then select the destination folder on your computer, enter a file name, and click Save. The
favorite will be saved as an .mpkminiplaymk3 file.

7

Loading Favorites
To load a favorite from a connected MPK mini Play mk3, click
the Get icon corresponding to the number of the desired favorite
(1-8).
Note: Using the software to edit a favorite loaded from your MPK
mini Play mk3 will not automatically affect this favorite on your
keyboard; you will have to send the edited favorite to the keyboard
after modifying it in order for the settings to take effect. See
Sending Favorites below to learn more.
To load a favorite from your computer, go to File > Open
Favorite. Alternatively, you can type Ctrl + O (Windows) or  + O
(Mac). Then locate the desired .mpkminiplaymk3 file and click
Open.

8

Sending Favorites
When you send from the Editor to a connected MPK mini Play mk3, all settings that you see in the
Editor will be stored as a favorite on the MPK mini Play mk3’s internal memory. When playing your
keyboard, the favorite will be available for you to activate whenever you like.
To send a Favorite to a connected MPK mini Play mk3, click the Send icon corresponding to the
number of the favorite you want to replace on your MPK mini Play mk3 (1-8). Doing so will overwrite
the favorite currently stored in this number slot on the MPK mini Play mk3.
Note: After sending a program to a Favorite, you will need to re-select it
from the hardware for the settings to take effect. Press and hold the
Favorites button and press the corresponding pad to reload the
Favorite with the new program.
You can also send a favorite to MPK mini Play mk3's RAM, where it will
be kept temporarily and will not overwrite any favorite stored on the
device. This applies the settings to the unit and lets you try them out
without fully committing to them. If you then decide that you like the
settings, you can save the favorite to your computer (see Saving
Favorites above) or overwrite one of the currently stored favorites on
your MPK mini Play mk3.
To send a Program to the RAM of a connected MPK mini Play mk3, click the SEND TO RAM
icon or go to File > Send Favorite > Ram.
Note: To avoid losing the settings kept in the RAM, you will need to save them before modifying
them with the Editor or exiting the Editor.

Editing a Pad’s MIDI Note
You can set the MIDI note for any of the pads in Bank A or Bank B. You can also set the channel
through which all the pads send their MIDI notes.
To select a pad’s MIDI note, click the Note field within the pad and then type the
desired MIDI note number (0 – 127). The MIDI note value will change accordingly.
The current MIDI note value (C-1 – G9) will be displayed on the pad.

To select the MIDI channel for all the pads, click the text field next
to Pad at the top left corner under MIDI Channel. Then, select the
desired MIDI channel number (1 – 16) from the drop-down menu.

To change the pads’ aftertouch type, click the field undertneath the pads labeled Global Pad
Aftertouch Type and select CHANNEL, POLY, or OFF. Enabling aftertouch allows for additional
MIDI messages to be sent via varying pressure from a pad after it has been pressed down.
CHANNEL mode affects the whole MIDI channel while POLY mode only affects the individual notes
pressed.

Editing a Knob
You can edit settings for each of the 4 knobs in both Bank A and Bank B, including CC message,
minimum value, and maximum value. You can also set the channel through which all the knobs send
MIDI data.
To edit the name of a knob, click the text field above the knob and type in the
desired name.
To select the CC message a knob sends, click the CC field under a knob and
then type the desired CC number (0 – 127).
To select a knob's minimum and maximum values, click the Min field
(minimum) or Max field (maximum) under a knob and then type the desired
value (0 – 127).

Note: Min/Max CC values will only be applied in MIDI Controller mode,
and will not affect internal sounds.
To select the MIDI channel for all the knobs, click the text field next to Keybed & Controls under
MIDI Channel at the top left corner of the window. Then select the desired MIDI channel number (1 –
16) from the drop-down menu. This will affect the X-Y Controller and keyboard as well as the knobs.

Editing the X-Y Controller (Joystick)
You can designate the function for each axis of the X-Y Controller (joystick), allowing you to use it
to manipulate up to two different parameters simultaneously.

To designate the function of the axis, click the X Axis or Y Axis field next to the X-Y Controller
icon and then select the desired function from the drop-down menu:
• Pitchbend: Moving the X-Y controller along this axis will bend the keyboard pitch.
• Single CC: Moving the X-Y controller along this axis will send a CC message.
• Dual CC: Moving the X-Y controller along this axis will send one CC message in the positive
direction (up or right) and another CC message in the negative direction (down or left).

To select the CC message the X-Y Controller sends (when set to Single CC or Dual CC), click
the CC field(s) and then type the desired value (0 – 127).
To select the MIDI channel for the X-Y Controller,
click the field next to Keybed & Controls under MIDI
Channel at the top left corner of the window. Then
select the desired MIDI channel number (1 – 16) from
the drop-down menu. This will affect the knobs and
keyboard as well as the X-Y Controller.

Editing the Keyboard
The Editor allows you to set the keyboard's octave and transposition.

To change the keyboard's octave, click the Octave field next to the keyboard icon. When the
drop-down menu appears, select the desired value (-4 to 4 octaves). An octave of 0 means the
keyboard is at its center/default octave.
To transpose the keyboard, click the Transpose field next to the keyboard icon. Type in the
desired value (-12 to 12 semitones). A transposition of 0 means the keyboard is not transposed.
Press the Edit Key Curve button to the right of the keyboard icon to open the Keybed Curve menu.
See Editing the Keybed Curve to learn more.

To select the MIDI channel for the Keyboard,
click the Keybed & Controls field under MIDI
Channel at the top of the window. Then select the
desired MIDI channel number (1 – 16) from the
drop-down menu. This will affect the knobs and
X-Y Controller as well as the keyboard.

Editing the Arpeggiator
You can edit various parameters for the arpeggiator.

To activate or deactivate the arpeggiator, click the On/Off icon. The icon will be light grey when
the arpeggiator is on and dark grey when it is off.
To set the arpeggiator's tempo, click the Tempo field and type in a tempo (60-240 BPM).
To set the arpeggiator's time division, click the Time Div field. When the drop-down menu
appears, select the desired setting (1/4 – 1/32T).
To set the arpeggiator's swing, click the Swing field and enter the desired percentage (50 – 75%);
the default value, 50%, is no swing.
To set the arpeggiator's octave range, click the Octave field and select the desired value from the
drop-down menu: 1, 2, 3, or 4 octaves.

14

To set the arpeggiator's mode, click the Mode field. When the drop-down menu appears, select
the desired setting. The mode determines how the arpeggiated notes are played back:
•

Up: Notes will sound from lowest to highest.

•

Down: Notes will sound from highest to lowest.

•

Exclusive: Notes will sound from lowest to highest and then back down. The lowest and
highest notes will sound only once at the directional change.

•

Inclusive: Notes will sound from lowest to highest and then back down. The lowest and
highest notes will sound twice at the directional change.

•

Order: Notes will sound in the order they were pressed.

•

Random: Notes will sound in random order.

To activate or deactivate the Latch, click the Latch field and then select Off or On from the dropdown menu.
•

Off: When the Latch is Off and you press keys, the Arpeggiator will cease after you
release your fingers from the keyboard.

•

On: When the Latch is On and you press keys, the Arpeggiator will continue even after
you release your fingers from the keyboard. While holding down the keys, you can add
more notes to the arpeggio by pressing down additional keys. If you press the keys,
release them, and then press a new combination of keys, the Arpeggiator will arpeggiate
the new notes.

To set the minimum number of taps required for MPK mini Play mk3’s Tap Tempo button to
detect a new arpeggiator tempo, click the Tempo Taps field and select the desired number from
the drop-down menu: 2, 3, or 4 taps.
Note: The Tap Tempo button is disabled if the Arpeggiator is synced to an external clock.

To set the clock destination of MPK mini Play mk3, click on the field next to Clock and
select either Internal or External from the drop-down menu. When set to Internal, the
arpeggiator will run off the unit’s internal clock. When set to External, the arpeggiator will run
off clock data sent from a connected DAW.

Editing the Sound
The Editor allows you to select the default keyboard sound and pad sound for a favorite.

To select the keyboard sound, Click the Keyboard Sound field at the top of the window. When the
drop-down menu appears, select one of the 128 available sounds.
To select the pad sound, Click the Pad Sound field at the top of the window. When the drop-down
menu appears, select one of the 10 available sounds.
To add additional effects to the selected Keyboard Sound, Click one of the effects fields under
Keyboard Sound FX and enter the desired value (0 – 127).

Filter selects a frequency above which all other frequencies will not pass through.
Reso. (Resonance) boosts the frequencies near the cutoff frequency (value selected by Filter field).
Attack controls the time it takes for a note to reach its maximum volume, starting from when the key
is pressed down.
Release controls the time it takes for a note to reach its minimum volume.
Reverb adds sustaining echo that simulates the sound reflecting off a solid surface.
Chorus makes duplicates of a sound to be played back slightly out of time with the input to create a
sweeping stereo effect.
EQ Low controls the amount of gain on the low-range frequencies of a sound.
EQ High controls the amount of gain on the high-range frequencies of a sound.

Editing the Keybed Curve
You can edit the keybed curve by going to File > Edit Key Curve or by pressing the button
reading Edit Key Curve to the right of the keyboard icon in the bottom right of the application
window.
This window allows you to adjust the velocity sensitivity of the keys to match the
responsiveness of your preferred instrument. If you usually play piano or keyboards with
weighted keys rather than synthesizers, you may prefer a heavier feeling key curve.

To make it easier to reach low velocity notes, decrease the value of data points 1 and 2.
To make it easier to reach high velocity notes, increase the value of data points 3 and 4.
To change the black key balance, click the field and enter a value from 0.5 to 2.0. The
chosen value is multiplied by the values selected above in the keybed curve to set the balance
between black and white keys accordingly.
To apply the changes you have made, you must press the SEND button.
To return to the default settings, press the SEND DEFAULTS button.
